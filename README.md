# 🏥 Healthcare Utilization & Claims Analysis

## Executive Summary

This project analyses healthcare utilisation and insurance claims data to support **evidence-based planning, system flow monitoring, and resource optimisation** within a healthcare setting. Using a dataset of **21,669 encounters across 3,286 patients**, the analysis focuses on payer mix, encounter drivers, utilisation trends over time, length of stay, and emergency department wait times.

The insights highlight a **high reliance on government-funded care**, a strong concentration of encounters linked to **chronic conditions**, and a utilisation pattern dominated by **ambulatory services**. These findings provide a data-driven foundation to support **planning, funding decisions, and service delivery improvements**, aligned with public health system objectives.

---

## Project Overview

### Objective
To extract, analyse, and visualise healthcare utilisation data in order to:
- Understand patient demand patterns
- Identify key cost and utilisation drivers
- Support planning and operational decision-making
- Highlight opportunities for improving system efficiency and outcomes

### Tools & Techniques
- Tableau for visual analytics and dashboarding  
- Structured healthcare dataset (21,669 records, 33 fields)  
- Quantitative analysis focusing on utilisation, outcomes, and system flow metrics  

### Key Metrics Analysed
- Total encounters and patient volume  
- Payer mix and funding distribution  
- Average Length of Stay (ALOS)  
- Emergency Room median wait time  
- Encounter trends over time  
- High-impact clinical encounter reasons  

---

## Detailed Analysis

### 1. Payer Mix & Funding Distribution

Medicare is the **dominant payer**, accounting for **7,006 encounters**, followed by Medicaid (3,125) and private insurers such as Humana and Blue Cross Blue Shield. This indicates that the patient population is largely composed of **elderly, disabled, or government-supported individuals**.

This payer mix highlights:
- Strong dependence on public healthcare funding
- The importance of accurate reporting for accountability and planning
- Sensitivity of service delivery to funding and policy changes

---

### 2. Clinical Drivers of Utilisation

**Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)** is the leading reason for encounters, with **9,128 encounters**, followed by pregnancy-related encounters and end-stage renal disease.

This pattern indicates:
- Sustained demand for **chronic disease management**
- Ongoing pressure on outpatient and specialist services
- The need for long-term, preventative planning rather than short-term capacity responses

---

### 3. Utilisation Trends Over Time

Ambulatory encounters show **consistently higher volumes** than other encounter types, peaking at **1,768 encounters in March**.

This suggests:
- Ambulatory care is the primary mode of service delivery
- Inpatient services are used more selectively
- Opportunities exist to strengthen outpatient pathways to reduce acute care pressure

---

### 4. System Flow & Operational Metrics

- **Average Length of Stay (ALOS): 0.2 days**  
  Reflects a system focused on short-stay and outpatient care rather than prolonged admissions.

- **Emergency Room Median Time: 120 minutes**  
  Indicates moderate waiting times, potentially influenced by chronic and complex cases.

Together, these metrics reflect a **high-volume, low-duration system**, emphasising the importance of efficient patient flow and timely care delivery.

---

### 5. Age Group Impact on Claims

Analysis by age group shows that **older age cohorts contribute disproportionately to claim volume and cost**, reinforcing the relationship between ageing populations, chronic conditions, and healthcare demand.

This insight supports the need for:
- Preventive and early-intervention strategies
- Community-based chronic care models
- Targeted funding allocation for high-impact demographic groups

---

## Key Takeaways

- Healthcare utilisation is largely driven by **chronic conditions**, particularly renal-related care  
- Government-funded payers dominate encounter volume, underscoring accountability and funding sensitivity  
- Ambulatory services represent the majority of system demand  
- Short lengths of stay combined with high encounter volumes indicate strong throughput requirements  
- Ageing populations are a key driver of utilisation and claim costs

## Recommendations

1. **Strengthen Chronic Disease Management**  
   Invest in preventive and community-based programmes to reduce repeat encounters and long-term system strain.

2. **Optimise Ambulatory Care Pathways**  
   Expand and coordinate outpatient services to maintain low inpatient utilisation while meeting rising demand.

3. **Support Data-Driven Planning & Funding**  
   Use payer mix and utilisation trends to inform funding models, service planning, and accountability reporting.

4. **Monitor System Flow Metrics Continuously**  
   Track ALOS and ER wait times to identify early signals of capacity pressure or service bottlenecks.

5. **Target High-Impact Demographics**  
   Develop tailored interventions for age groups that contribute most to utilisation and claim costs.

- Inform evidence-based decision-making
- Support planning, funding, and outcome monitoring
- Improve transparency and accountability in healthcare delivery
- Enable system-level insights across complex health services
